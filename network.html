Competitiveness Action Network

Sustaining the momentum generated during the Academy through a single statewide learning and implementation network.

Purpose

The purpose of the Competitiveness Action Network is to sustain the momentum generated during the Academy by connecting regional leadership teams, accelerating implementation of their Competitiveness Action Plans, sharing innovations across regions, and creating a statewide community of practice dedicated to increasing Tennessee's long-term prosperity.

Rather than operating independently, the regional delegations become members of a single statewide learning and implementation network.

Program Objectives

The Network will:

  • Advise on successful implementation of each Regional Competitiveness Action Plan.
  • Facilitate collaboration across Tennessee's regions.
  • Share best practices and lessons learned.
  • Connect delegations with faculty, subject experts, and statewide resources.
  • Track implementation progress through US Cluster Mapping measures.
  • Develop future regional leadership.
  • Build a durable statewide competitiveness movement.

Program Components

I. Resources Online

1. Regional Collaboration Workspace

Each region will receive a dedicated online workspace in the Statewide Collaboration Hub described below. The Regional Workspace will contain:

  • Final Competitiveness Action Plan
  • Working documents
  • Meeting notes
  • Research
  • Regional data
  • Presentations
  • Resource library
  • Calendar
  • Contact directory

Members can continue refining initiatives in real time.

2. Statewide Collaboration Hub

A statewide Google Workspace (or equivalent platform) will include:

Discussion Forums

  • Questions
  • Ideas
  • Funding opportunities
  • Policy updates
  • Research
  • Partnership opportunities
  • Success stories
  • Lessons learned

Shared Resource Library

  • Regional Initiative Inventories
  • Case studies (Binder)
  • Cluster research
  • Innovation tools
  • Templates
  • Speaker presentations

Cross-Regional Communities

Members working on similar initiatives can collaborate regardless of geography. Possible statewide working groups include:

  • Innovation Ecosystems
  • Research, Tech Transfer, and University-Business Engagement (Tennessee Tech Transfer and Cluster Economic Development Council)
  • Advanced Manufacturing
  • Nuclear
  • Life Sciences
  • Workforce
  • AI
  • Rural Development
  • Entrepreneurship
  • Housing
  • Infrastructure
  • Higher Education Partnerships (Tennessee University Presidents Council)

3. Academy Photo and Media Archive

Participants will receive access to:

  • Professional and participant photographs
  • Video highlights
  • Speaker presentations
  • Participant directory
  • Regional presentations
  • Media coverage

This archive also becomes an orientation resource for future Fellows.

II. Collaboration Events

1. Quarterly Statewide Implementation Forums

Quarterly virtual meetings will include:

  • Regional implementation updates
  • Challenges requiring assistance
  • Emerging opportunities
  • Policy developments
  • New funding sources
  • Guest experts
  • Faculty insights
  • Cross-regional collaboration opportunities

Each meeting should feature two or three regional case studies highlighting successful implementation.

Every six months, the quarterly virtual meetings will feature a concise implementation update including:

  • Accomplishments
  • Milestones reached
  • Projects launched
  • Partners added
  • Funding secured
  • Obstacles encountered
  • Assistance requested
  • Next priorities

Reports will be shared statewide to encourage learning between regions.

2. Statewide Expert Network

Develop a directory of experts willing to assist regions. Potential expertise includes:

  • Economic development
  • Site selection
  • Capital formation
  • Workforce
  • Technology commercialization
  • Research universities
  • Infrastructure
  • Rural development
  • Housing
  • Transportation
  • Tourism
  • Healthcare
  • Advanced manufacturing
  • Federal grants
  • International investment

3. Regional Peer Reviews

Each region partners with another region annually to encourage progress. Peer reviews provide:

  • Constructive feedback
  • Fresh ideas
  • Outside perspective
  • Collaboration opportunities
  • Accountability

This model has proven effective in many regional economic development networks because it encourages learning without creating a top-down evaluation process.

4. Competitiveness Scorecard, Regional Factbook, and Inventories Input and Refinement

Collaborate with GAP and the US Cluster Mapping Project to customize metrics for each of the regions based on the cluster strategy of each region.

The scorecard and factbooks focus primarily on implementation milestones during the first several years, while longer-term economic outcomes naturally emerge over time.

Collaborate with GAP and ORAU to refine and update the Regional Initiative Inventories.

5. Nomination and Induction of Academy Leadership

Each regional delegation becomes the steward of its region's Academy. Responsibilities include:

  • Recruiting the next delegation
  • Mentoring new Fellows
  • Recommending faculty
  • Suggesting Academy improvements
  • Helping shape the next regional agenda
  • Ensuring continuity between Academy cohorts

At each new Academy, the program opens with a "showcase" in which new delegations present updates as a basis for new work (the update includes a brief written report) including:

  • Regional accomplishments
  • Share lessons learned
  • Recognize outstanding initiatives
  • Launch new statewide collaborations
  • Introduce the incoming Academy Fellows

This opening Academy event creates continuity between Academy classes and a self-renewing leadership pipeline rather than starting from scratch each year.
